# An Open Engineering Community Manifest

*Abstract*
This is the manifest of a community framework to create or run, rationalise, and manage an engineering community within a bigger organisation, make it collaborative, business relevant and visible.

## North Star Governing Principle

Governance: The Governing Board will hold responsibility to the [Engineering Organisation | Office of the CTO | CTO] to ensure that the North Star Governing Principle is regularly reviewed for modifications or updates, and that these are handled in a timely manner.

### Mission

Support the success of your company's transformation, openness and collaboration goals, etc, e.g.
> Support the success of the E.ONs DevOps transformation.

### Statement

[COMPANY] has identified [X] main goals to improve business in the areas of [foo] and [bar]. The Engineering team and especially the Engineering community contributes to these goals through being a welcoming best-in-class place to work for engineers and the development of their careers.

> E.ON has identified 4 main goals in their DevOps transformation (Project Nautilus) in the areas of product centricity, automation, DevOps processing and rightsourcing. The engineering org has operational as well as technological responsibility in this journey, and the engineering community is the backbone to its success.

### Rationale

An engineering team is more than the sum of its members technological expertise. To build and sustain an attractive and collaborative team, whose members see and use ubiqitous opportunities to develop, it's essential to have a healthy community with a value-based culture.

### Implications

A vivid, diverse and psychologically safe community is built on top of a growth mindset, sustainability, togetherness, belonging and many more factors, that, in turn, contribute to the team's performance and retention, and the overall team purpose and its role in the company's transformation. To ensure all of this to not only happen but develop further and improve, we're defining **5 Guiding Community Principles**: **Openness**, **Kindness**, **Simplicity**, **Inclusivity** and **Ubiquitous Learning**.

## Community Principles

**Statement** - The following principles are intended to guide the evolution and growth of the engineering community within the company.

### Principle #1 - Openness by default

**Statement** -
Design and engineer for open collaboration, patterns and practices

**Rationale** -
The more open we work, the easier our work is seen, adopted, and improved. This leads to faster development, and also community growth.

**Implications** -
We're setting the norm to allow internal and external collaborators to have access to all information that may be relevant today or tomorrow.

    All collaborators shall have
    - free access to project documentation
    - free access to code
    - free access to meetings and group conversations, their transcripts and recordings
Reasons to undisclose information exist, but should be limited to:

- *protecting the confidence of a partner/customer/colleague*
- *personnel information*
- *data protection, e.g. GDPR*

### Principle #2 - Kindness by design

**Statement** -
Assume positive intent, respect others' views, be ready to help

**Rationale** -
Giving others around us the benefit of the doubt reduces unnecessary tension and fosters a supportive work environment. Respecting opinions that diverge from our own creates constructive conversations and lead to mutual understanding and eventually stronger relationships. A supportive environment strengthens morale and enhances problem-solving.

**Implications** -
We have defined a Community Code of Conduct that puts down the bare minimum of our rules of collaboration, so everyone has a common understanding and something to rely on in case of an actual incident. Beyond what is written down in this Community Code of Conduct, everyone is expected to make use of the positive effects of everyday kindness and lead by example in assuming best intent, to let others rise to your view of them.

### Principle #3 - Design for inclusivity

**Statement** -
Diversity, equity, inclusion and belonging is a top priority and always top of mind.

**Rationale** -
Diversity, equity and inclusion has to be an integral part of all our concepts from the very beginning and cannot be bolted on afterwards.

**Implications** -
In a world where our talents are our most precious assets, we can't afford to exlcude groups of people, marginalise them or otherwise unintentionally ignore any parts of the global workforce. Instead we want to seek out otherness, avoid limitations of sameness and strive to create teams that reach the highest level of creativity and performance through their diversity.

### Principle #4 - Never stop learning

**Statement** -
Interpret every success and every failure an opportunity to learn. And make them happen.

**Rationale** -
Learning empowers us to grow, adapt, and thrive. Abilities and intelligence can be developed through effort and perseverance. Life-long learning is not an option, it's a requirement of our culture, time and industry.
Learning from failure is easier than learning from success. Yet we strive to only succeed, and fail as little as possible. To never stop learning, failing will often need to be actively provoked.

**Implications**
We'll need ot actively overcome fear of failure, as it affects personal growth. Societal pressure, judgment and a desire for perfection often stand in our way learn.
We're cultivating a community growth mindset, where we counter this fear by celebrating failure the same way we celebrate success.

### Principle #5 - Simplify by design

**Statement** -
Design and engineer for simplicity.

**Rationale** -
Simple concepts and programs are easier to communicate, build, deploy, operate, and evolve thus requiring lower costs.

**Implications** -
Simple is hard and requires a better understanding of the problem space, available technologies, experience with previous designs to eliminate unneeded complexity and ongoing effort to fight entropy over time.
Avoid over-engineering today to pay off in the future at the same time balance to provide for the easy integration of near term capabilities.
Avoid premature optimization and allow the design to emerge from known implementations.
Focus on programs that are directly supporting functionality that provides value that is differentiating.
Strive for the Simplest Option:
Of two otherwise equal solutions, the simpler is usually better.
